\begin{abstract}
Let \(\Omega\subset\R^2\) be a bounded billiard table. We separate four
objects that are often conflated: the continuous billiard flow, its
collision-section reduction, a deterministic observation of collision
data, and a finite or stochastic recorded channel. On the regular
collision space
\[
\Mcal_\partial=\{(s,\varphi):s\in\partial\Omega_{\rm reg},
\ |\varphi|<\pi/2\},
\]
the billiard map is invertible almost everywhere and preserves
\[
\dd\mu_\partial
=\frac{\cos\varphi}{2|\partial\Omega|}\,\dd s\,\dd\varphi
=\frac{1}{2|\partial\Omega|}\,\dd s\,\dd p,
\qquad p=\sin\varphi.
\]
Therefore the impact map itself is not an information-loss map.
Loss occurs only after a declared projection, partition, finite
horizon, missing-event policy, or stochastic channel. For a known
strictly convex table, two consecutive regular impact positions
recover the outgoing direction, whereas one impact position does not.
Finite symbolic words are necessarily non-injective on a continuum
of initial states, but an infinite coding may be generating; marginal
symbol entropy is neither entropy rate nor Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy.
For the quantum layer, Laplacian eigenvalues
\(\lambda_n\) have dimension \(L^{-2}\), while physical energies are
\[
E_n=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\lambda_n.
\]
Rigid-motion invariance, similarity covariance, Weyl recovery of area,
and isospectral non-identifiability are stated with their hypotheses.
The result is a strict observation interface and reproducible
benchmark, not a new billiard theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, conventions, and claim firewall}

\subsection{Regular table class}

Unless a stronger hypothesis is stated, \(\Omega\subset\R^2\) is
bounded, connected, and open, with boundary equal to a finite union of
piecewise \(C^3\) arcs. The set of corners is
\(\partial\Omega_{\rm sing}\); the regular part is
\(\partial\Omega_{\rm reg}\). A trajectory hitting a corner, grazing
the boundary, or accumulating infinitely many collisions in finite
time is outside the default regular orbit class. Convexity, strict
convexity, dispersing curvature, focusing curvature, and finite
horizon are additional hypotheses, never defaults.

The dimensional model has a declared speed \(c_0>0\). A regular hidden
state is
\[
z=(q,u)\in X:=\Omega\times\Sph^1,\qquad
v=c_0u,\qquad \norm{u}=1.
\]
Between collisions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:free-flight}
\dot q=c_0u,\qquad \dot u=0.
\end{equation}
At \(q\in\partial\Omega_{\rm reg}\), let \(n(q)\) be the inward unit
normal. If \(u^-\cdot n<0\), elastic specular reflection is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflection}
u^+=u^- -2(u^-\cdot n)n.
\end{equation}
The convention is part of the model: changing to an outward normal
changes the inequalities but not the physical reflected vector.

\begin{warning}[No universal billiard claim]
Properties of circular, elliptic, dispersing, polygonal, and stadium
billiards are not interchangeable. In particular, ergodicity,
mixing, positive Lyapunov exponents, decay of correlations, and
integrability require separate table classes and hypotheses.
\end{warning}

\subsection{Typed observation chain}

For a protocol record \(\lambda\), the complete chain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observation-chain}
X\xrightarrow{\Scal}\Mcal_\partial
\xrightarrow{\Ocal_\lambda}Z_\lambda
\xrightarrow{\Kcal_\lambda}
\operatorname{Law}(Y_\lambda\mid Z_\lambda)
\xrightarrow{\widehat\theta_\lambda}\widehat\theta.
\end{equation}
Here \(\Scal\) is collision-section reduction, not a sensor;
\(\Ocal_\lambda\) is deterministic; \(\Kcal_\lambda\) is either a
declared stochastic channel or the identity kernel; and
\(\widehat\theta_\lambda\) is an estimator or direct readout. The
continuous flow is related to the collision map by a roof function
and is not identified with the discrete map.

\section{Collision space, singularities, and invariant measure}

Parameterize each regular boundary component by arclength \(s\), with
positively oriented unit tangent \(t(s)\). For an outgoing unit vector
write
\[
u=\cos\varphi\,n(s)+\sin\varphi\,t(s),\qquad
\varphi\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2),
\]
and set \(p=\sin\varphi\in(-1,1)\).

\begin{definition}[Regular collision space and map]
\[
\Mcal_\partial
=\{(s,\varphi):s\in\partial\Omega_{\rm reg},
\ |\varphi|<\pi/2\}.
\]
Let \(\Scal_1\) contain states whose next collision is a corner,
grazing, non-unique, or undefined. The collision map is
\[
T:\Mcal_\partial\setminus\Scal_1\longrightarrow\Mcal_\partial,
\qquad T(s,\varphi)=(s',\varphi').
\]
The roof function
\[
\tau(s,\varphi)
=\frac{\norm{q(s')-q(s)}}{c_0}>0
\]
is the physical flight time to the next collision.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Regular invertibility and invariant measure]
\label{prop:collision-measure}
For the reversible elastic billiard, \(T\) is invertible away from
forward and backward singularity sets. It preserves the two-form
\[
\omega_\partial=\cos\varphi\,\dd s\wedge\dd\varphi
=\dd s\wedge\dd p
\]
and the normalized probability measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:collision-measure}
\dd\mu_\partial
=\frac{\cos\varphi}{2L_\partial}\,\dd s\,\dd\varphi
=\frac{1}{2L_\partial}\,\dd s\,\dd p,
\qquad L_\partial:=|\partial\Omega|.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Specular reflection is time reversible, so reversing the outgoing
velocity at a regular collision supplies the inverse branch. The
preservation of \(\omega_\partial\) is the standard Poincare-section
restriction of Liouville measure for billiards. Normalization is
direct:
\[
\frac1{2L_\partial}
\int_{\partial\Omega}\!\dd s
\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\cos\varphi\,\dd\varphi=1.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{warning}[Reduction is not observation loss]
The full pair \((s_n,p_n)\) evolves under an almost-everywhere
invertible, measure-preserving map. Calling \(T\) itself a compression
is incorrect. The flow requires the roof \(\tau\), and information
loss must be assigned to a subsequent observation channel.
\end{warning}

\section{Impact-position observation and identifiability}

Define the instantaneous position readout
\[
\pi_s:\Mcal_\partial\to\partial\Omega_{\rm reg},
\qquad \pi_s(s,p)=s.
\]
This map is non-injective because all \(p\in(-1,1)\) share the same
output at fixed \(s\). Sequential observations have different
identifiability.

\begin{proposition}[Two-impact reconstruction]\label{prop:two-impact}
Let \(\Omega\) be a known strictly convex table with \(C^1\) boundary.
For two distinct consecutive regular impact points
\[
q_n=q(s_n),\qquad q_{n+1}=q(s_{n+1}),
\]
the outgoing direction and collision coordinate at \(q_n\) are
uniquely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-impact-reconstruction}
u_n^+
=\frac{q_{n+1}-q_n}{\norm{q_{n+1}-q_n}},
\qquad
p_n=u_n^+\cdot t(s_n).
\end{equation}
Thus the two-step position observation
\((s_n,s_{n+1})\) reconstructs \((s_n,p_n)\) on the stated regular
class.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Free flight is a straight segment and strict convexity makes the open
chord lie in \(\Omega\). Its oriented endpoints therefore determine
the unit direction. Projection on the declared tangent gives \(p_n\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Unknown geometry, unordered impacts, missed impacts, coincident
points, finite spatial resolution, non-convex visibility, and
stochastic misclassification enlarge the observation fiber. The
proposition does not survive those changes without additional
hypotheses.
\end{remark}

For a horizon \(N\), useful deterministic channels are
\begin{align}
\Ocal_{\rm full,N}(z)&=(s_k,p_k)_{k=0}^{N-1},\label{eq:full-channel}\\
\Ocal_{\rm pos,N}(z)&=(s_k)_{k=0}^{N-1},\label{eq:position-channel}\\
\Ocal_{\alpha,N}(z)&=(a_k)_{k=0}^{N-1},
\qquad a_k=\alpha(s_k),\label{eq:symbol-channel}
\end{align}
where \(\alpha:\partial\Omega\to\{1,\ldots,m\}\) is a declared
measurable partition with a boundary-assignment rule.

\section{Finite symbolic channels and entropy semantics}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-horizon non-injectivity]
\label{prop:finite-symbol}
Let \(U\subset\Mcal_\partial\) be uncountable and suppose
\(\Ocal_{\alpha,N}\) is defined on \(U\), with \(m<\infty\) and
\(N<\infty\). Then
\[
\Ocal_{\alpha,N}:U\to\{1,\ldots,m\}^N
\]
is not injective.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The codomain contains at most \(m^N\) words, while \(U\) is
uncountable.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not imply that every infinite symbolic coding
is lossy. A generating partition may identify almost every orbit from
its bi-infinite itinerary, modulo partition boundaries.

Let \(Z_0\sim\mu_\partial\), \(Z_k=T^kZ_0\), and
\(A_k=\alpha(\pi_s Z_k)\). The process is stationary. For a logarithm
base \(b>1\), define
\begin{align}
H_b(A_0)
&=-\sum_{j:p_j>0}p_j\log_b p_j,\label{eq:marginal-entropy}\\
H_b(A_0^{n-1})
&=-\sum_{w:P_n(w)>0}P_n(w)\log_bP_n(w),\label{eq:block-entropy}\\
h_b(T,\alpha)
&=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n H_b(A_0^{n-1}),\label{eq:entropy-rate}
\end{align}
where the last limit exists for a stationary finite-alphabet process.
The Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy is
\[
h_{\mu_\partial,b}(T)=\sup_\alpha h_b(T,\alpha).
\]
Equality for one partition requires a generating hypothesis.

\begin{warning}[Marginal entropy is not dynamical entropy]
The empirical quantity
\[
\widehat H_b=-\sum_j\widehat p_j\log_b\widehat p_j
\]
uses only one-symbol frequencies. It ignores order, is
partition-dependent, and is neither an entropy-rate estimate nor a
KS-entropy estimate without an explicit stochastic and asymptotic
protocol.
\end{warning}

\section{Exact integrable reference: the disk}

Let \(\Omega=B_R(0)\), \(L_\partial=2\pi R\), and retain
\(p=\sin\varphi\).

\begin{theorem}[Disk collision map]\label{thm:disk-map}
On the regular disk collision space,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk-map}
T_R(s,p)
=\left(s+2R\arccos p\pmod{2\pi R},\,p\right).
\end{equation}
The reduced angular-momentum coordinate
\[
j_z:=q\times u=Rp
\]
is conserved and has the dimension of length.  The physical specific
angular momentum is \(q\times v=c_0j_z\).  The rotation number and roof
time are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:disk-readouts}
\rho(p)=\frac{\arccos p}{\pi}\pmod1,\qquad
\tau_R(p)=\frac{2R\sqrt{1-p^2}}{c_0}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At a collision, reflection reverses the normal component of \(u\) and
preserves its tangential component \(p\). Since \(q=Re_r\),
\(q\times u=R(u\cdot e_\theta)=Rp\), while multiplication by \(c_0\)
restores the physical specific angular momentum. The chord subtends
the central angle \(\pi-2\varphi=2\arccos p\), giving
\cref{eq:disk-map}. Its length is
\(2R\cos\varphi=2R\sqrt{1-p^2}\), which gives the roof.
\end{proof}

The disk is an exact benchmark, not evidence that a generic billiard
is integrable. For an ellipse, tangency to a member of a confocal
caustic family is a classical invariant on regular trajectories; this
statement is retained only as a standard example and is not extended
to arbitrary convex tables.

\section{Chaotic tables and finite-time diagnostics}

The Bunimovich stadium is a standard focusing table for which
ergodicity and hyperbolic behavior are classical results under the
stadium hypotheses. Those results do not justify reading a Lyapunov
exponent from an arbitrary two-trajectory plot.

For \(z\) outside singularities and a tangent vector \(\xi\ne0\), the
mathematical exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lyapunov}
\lambda_+(z,\xi)
=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N
\log\frac{\norm{D T_z^N\xi}}{\norm{\xi}},
\end{equation}
when the limit exists. A finite-window diagnostic must instead be
declared as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-time-proxy}
\widehat\lambda_{N_0,N_1}
=\frac{\sum_{k=N_0}^{N_1}w_k(k-\bar k)
\log(d_k/d_*)}
{\sum_{k=N_0}^{N_1}w_k(k-\bar k)^2},
\end{equation}
where \(d_k\) is computed in a declared collision-space metric,
\(d_*>0\) is a reference distance, \(w_k\ge0\), and
\(\bar k=\sum w_kk/\sum w_k\). The fit must state the window,
renormalization rule, saturation cutoff, singularity handling, and
uncertainty model. Without those fields,
\(\widehat\lambda\) is only a plot slope.

\begin{warning}[No chaos from one positive fit]
A positive finite-window slope does not prove a positive asymptotic
Lyapunov exponent, ergodicity, mixing, Bernoulli behavior, or positive
KS entropy. These are distinct claims.
\end{warning}

\section{Quantum billiards: operator, dimensions, and spectrum}

For a bounded open \(\Omega\), define the Dirichlet quadratic form
\[
q_\Omega[\psi]=\int_\Omega\norm{\nabla\psi}^2\,\dd x,
\qquad \psi\in H_0^1(\Omega).
\]
Its Friedrichs extension is the nonnegative self-adjoint Dirichlet
Laplacian \(-\Delta_\Omega^D\). It has compact resolvent and spectrum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:laplacian-spectrum}
0<\lambda_1(\Omega)\le\lambda_2(\Omega)\le\cdots,
\qquad \lambda_n\to\infty,
\qquad [\lambda_n]=L^{-2},
\end{equation}
with multiplicity.

\begin{proposition}[Laplacian-to-energy bridge]
\label{prop:energy-bridge}
For a nonrelativistic particle of declared mass \(m>0\),
\[
\widehat H_\Omega
=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\Delta_\Omega^D,
\qquad
E_n=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\lambda_n.
\]
Thus \(\lambda_n\) is not an energy. The bridge has SI dimension
\[
\frac{[\hbar]^2[\lambda_n]}{[m]}
=\frac{(ML^2T^{-1})^2L^{-2}}{M}
=ML^2T^{-2}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Rigid invariance and scale covariance]
\label{prop:spectral-covariance}
For a Euclidean isometry \(g\) and scale \(a>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-scaling}
\lambda_n(g\Omega)=\lambda_n(\Omega),\qquad
\lambda_n(a\Omega)=a^{-2}\lambda_n(\Omega).
\end{equation}
Consequently, in two dimensions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dimensionless-spectrum}
\widetilde\lambda_n(\Omega):=|\Omega|\lambda_n(\Omega)
\end{equation}
is invariant under similarities.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Rigid motions induce a unitary pullback on \(L^2\). Under
\(x=ay\), the Laplacian becomes \(a^{-2}\Delta_y\), while
\(|a\Omega|=a^2|\Omega|\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Weyl area readout]\label{thm:weyl}
Let \(N_\Omega(\Lambda)=\#\{n:\lambda_n<\Lambda\}\). For a planar open
set of finite area,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl}
N_\Omega(\Lambda)
\sim\frac{|\Omega|}{4\pi}\Lambda
\qquad(\Lambda\to\infty).
\end{equation}
Hence the full Dirichlet spectrum determines area. Boundary-length
terms require additional boundary regularity and a stated asymptotic
theorem; they are not inferred from \cref{eq:weyl} alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral non-identifiability]\label{thm:isospectral}
There exist noncongruent planar domains with the same Dirichlet
spectrum, including multiplicities. Therefore
\[
\Ocal_{\rm spec}:\Omega\longmapsto(\lambda_n(\Omega))_{n\ge1}
\]
is not injective on the class of all planar domains.
\end{theorem}

\begin{warning}[Classical--quantum firewall]
Sharing the domain \(\Omega\) does not identify the classical
collision map with the quantum spectral map. Any semiclassical claim
requires an explicit limit, observable class, and theorem. No
solution of inverse spectral geometry or quantum chaos is claimed
here.
\end{warning}

\section{Dissipative and non-specular variants}

The reference dynamics is elastic. A normal restitution model with
\(e\in[0,1]\), unchanged tangential velocity, and particle mass
\(m>0\) gives
\[
v_n^+=-e\,v_n^-,
\qquad
\Delta E_{\rm diss}
=\frac m2(1-e^2)(v_n^-)^2\ge0.
\]
If \(e<1\), speed is no longer constant, so
\((s,\varphi)\) is not a closed state space: speed or energy must be
added. Friction, stochastic reflection, rough walls, moving
boundaries, and compliant impact each require a new channel and
constitutive law. The invariant measure
\cref{eq:collision-measure} is not inherited automatically.

\section{Observation invariants and protocol requirements}

\begin{definition}[Recoverable quantity]
Let \(\Ocal:X\to Y\). A quantity \(I:X\to V\) is exactly recoverable
from \(\Ocal\) if there exists \(\widetilde I:Y\to V\) such that
\[
I=\widetilde I\circ\Ocal.
\]
Equivalently, \(I\) is constant on every observation fiber
\(\Ocal^{-1}(y)\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Group invariant readout]
For a group \(G\curvearrowright X\), a readout \(I:X\to V\) is
\(G\)-invariant when \(I(gx)=I(x)\) for all admissible \(g,x\).
Equivariance, scale covariance, and estimator stability are not
called invariance.
\end{definition}

A complete billiard protocol records at least:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
\item table class, boundary regularity, corner and grazing policy;
\item boundary parameterization, tangent and normal orientation;
\item speed or energy convention, time unit, and reference frame;
\item collision section, roof-time treatment, and observation horizon;
\item deterministic readout, partition, spatial and temporal resolution;
\item missing-impact, duplicate-impact, and misclassification policy;
\item stochastic kernel or explicit noiseless assumption;
\item log base, metric, regression window, weights, and zero guards;
\item spectral boundary condition, multiplicity convention, mass, and
      \(\hbar\) convention whenever energy is reported;
\item estimator, uncertainty model, and declared exactness class.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducible benchmarks}

The release includes executable tests and a machine-readable benchmark
table. The exact reference cases are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=3pt]
\item normalization of \(\mu_\partial\) and the change
      \(p=\sin\varphi\);
\item invertibility, conserved \(p\), roof time, and rotation number
      for the disk map;
\item reconstruction of \(p_n\) from two consecutive impacts in a
      strictly convex disk;
\item two cyclic binary words with identical marginal entropy but
      different conditional transition entropy;
\item exact rectangular Dirichlet eigenvalues, \(a^{-2}\) spectral
      scaling, and invariance of \(|\Omega|\lambda_n\);
\item the dimensional bridge
      \(E_n=\hbar^2\lambda_n/(2m)\);
\item finite-word non-injectivity and zero-denominator guards.
\end{enumerate}

For the declared disk benchmark \(R=2.3\,{\rm m}\), \(p=0.4\), and
\(c_0=3\,{\rm m\,s^{-1}}\),
\[
\rho=\frac{\arccos(0.4)}{\pi}\approx0.36901012,\qquad
\tau\approx1.40532321\,{\rm s}.
\]
For the cyclic words \(01010101\) and \(00110011\), both marginal
entropies equal one bit, while their empirical first-order
conditional entropies are respectively zero and one bit. This is an
exact order-sensitivity benchmark, not a chaos diagnostic.
