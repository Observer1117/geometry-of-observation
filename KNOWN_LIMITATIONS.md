# Known limitations — v1.3.0

1. **No external peer review.** The release is an internally validated research corpus/preprint, not a peer-reviewed publication.
2. **No global physical-equivalence claim.** Shared typed interfaces across modules do not imply that the represented physical systems are dynamically, empirically, or ontologically equivalent.
3. **Mixed novelty status.** Much of the corpus is normative infrastructure, synthesis, or domain adaptation. A module is not a novel subject-matter theorem merely because it has been retyped in the common framework.
4. **Validation scope.** The executable checks verify declared contracts, benchmarks, hashes, dependency structure, and selected counterexamples. They do not mechanically prove every mathematical statement.
5. **Literature completeness.** References establish principal boundaries and precedents but do not certify an exhaustive novelty search across all relevant literature.
6. **Application dependence.** Operational conclusions depend on specified state spaces, observation channels, groups/actions, sampling protocols, thresholds, partitions, synchronization rules, and noise models.
7. **Finite numerical evidence.** Numerical benchmarks support implementation correctness for the tested cases; they do not establish unrestricted analytic theorems.
8. **Frozen licensing.** CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 permits sharing of the unadapted material under its terms but restricts distribution of adaptations. Researchers should cite the corpus and publish independent extensions without presenting modified corpus files as part of v1.3.0.
9. **Journal unit.** The 166-page master is an archival compendium. Formal peer review should proceed through compact articles built around explicit central theorems, proofs, counterexamples, and literature comparisons.

These limitations are part of the release boundary and must not be omitted in summaries of the corpus.
